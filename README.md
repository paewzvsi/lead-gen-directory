## The Angie's List Landscape: Who's Actually Out There

Before you can build something better, you have to know what you're competing with. Here's the field, stripped down to what matters.

**The big pay-per-lead marketplaces**

- **Angi / Angie's List** — the original. Subscription tiers ($0–$350/month) plus $15–$100+ per lead. Shared leads, household-name trust, increasingly ad-heavy.
- **HomeAdvisor** — owned by the same parent company as Angi. Geared toward larger projects. Same shared-lead model, same cost complaints, same FTC settlement.
- **Thumbtack** — pay-per-lead ($10–$80+), but contractors review job details before paying. More control, less volume predictability.
- **Networx** — shared leads, $14–$100/lead. Often used as a secondary source to compare ROI.
- **HomeGuide** — quote-based marketplace, ~$20–$75/lead. Good for cost-conscious pros willing to compete on bids.
- **Bark** — credit-based, ~$5–$36/lead. Pros choose which requests to pursue. Slightly broader category mix than pure home services.

**The discovery and inbound platforms**

- **Houzz / Houzz Pro** — portfolio-driven. ~$249/month for project management software, $499+/month for the advertising add-on. Sells visual trust, not raw lead flow.
- **Yelp** — cost-per-click, monthly budget model. Strong for review-rich businesses doing "near me" local searches. Harder to track true cost per booked job.
- **Nextdoor** — neighborhood word-of-mouth. Free, hyperlocal, no formal vetting. Good for reputation, weak for predictable volume.
- **BuildZoom** — acquired by Block Renovation. Weaves permit histories and licensing data into its matching. A more quantitative angle on contractor vetting.

**The structured and franchise models**

- **Block Renovation** — quiz-based matching for medium-to-large remodels. Pre-vetted contractor network, dedicated project planners, milestone-based payments. Free for homeowners, focused on quality over quantity.
- **Google Local Services Ads** — exclusive leads, $20–$150+/lead, requires Google Screened badge. Probably the highest-intent option on the list.
- **Home Depot Pro Referral** — points-based referrals tied to in-store material purchases. No per-lead cost, but limited to retail-aligned installs.
- **Neighborly** — franchise model. You buy into a brand and get territory-based leads. Not really a lead platform — it's a business-in-a-box.

If you stare at this list long enough, a pattern emerges. Every single one of these companies is, at its core, **a directory with a monetization layer bolted on top.** Some charge the homeowner, some charge the contractor, some charge both. Some vet, some don't. Some specialize in a niche (Block for renovations, Houzz for design-forward work), some try to be everything to everyone (Angi, Thumbtack).

Which means the barrier to entry for "the next angie's list similar company" isn't a technical problem anymore. It's a niche problem. Pick the right slice — wedding vendors in your state, HVAC contractors in the Midwest, mobile dog groomers in the Sun Belt — and the directory software to actually run it costs less than a single Angi lead campaign.
